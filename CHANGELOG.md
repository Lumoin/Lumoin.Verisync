## [0.0.8] - 2026-08-15

### Changed

- `ObserveMemberVersionDelegate` answers with a `MemberVersionReport` — the version beside the identity the answering host asserts for itself — and `ReadReadinessAsync` refuses an answer naming another member instead of counting it or reporting it unreachable. The readiness report is counted over distinct members of the membership it measures, reached through an endpoint map a deployment wires by hand, and two probe routes landing on one host would let one replica fill two slots and a decommission gate clear on fewer distinct replicas than it claims; the record path has refused exactly that wiring error at its quorum since the reply gained its recorder field, and the one probe the decommission runbook rests on now refuses it too. The identity is the answering host's own claim and is not authentication. **Breaking** for every implementation of the delegate and for every caller that read its result as a bare version.

- The value-codec seam is one named pair rather than naked delegates, and it is format-agnostic: `WriteValueDelegate<TWriter, TValue>` and `ReadValueDelegate<TSource, TValue>` in the core name the seam every codec factory takes, with the format entering only through the writer or source type argument — `Utf8JsonWriter` and `JsonElement` at the JSON factories, `CborWriter` and `CborReader` at the CBOR channel — the way `SerializeMessageDelegate<TMessage>` already keeps the transport seam serialization-agnostic one layer up. Every factory across `CrdtStateJson`, `ConsensusMessageJson`, `QuePaxaMessageJson`, `RaftJson`, `ReconciliationJson`, `LogCommitmentJson` and `CborChannelSerialization` takes the named pair where it took a naked `Action`/`Func`, and `QuePaxaMessageJson.CreateVersionedValueWriter` and `CreateVersionedValueReader` return it where they returned the naked types, so the record codec they build composes into the envelope factories without a conversion. A future encoding reuses the same pair rather than minting its own. **Breaking** for a caller that stored a factory argument or result in an explicitly typed `Action`/`Func` variable; a lambda or method-group argument binds unchanged.

- The CASPaxos change function is the named `ChangeRegisterValueDelegate<TValue>` on `CasPaxosRegister<TValue>.Change`, `FastCasPaxosRegister<TValue>.Recover` and `FastProposer<TValue>.RecoverAsync`, where each took a naked `Func<TValue?, TValue>`. It is deliberately a distinct type from `ComputeRegisterValueDelegate<TValue>` despite the identical shape, because the two run on opposite sides of the round and substituting one for the other is the confusion the surface documents as unsafe: a CASPaxos change composes with the value the round recovered, so intent survives contention, while a QuePaxa recompute runs outside the round and re-proposes whole. **Breaking** for a caller that passed an explicitly typed `Func<TValue?, TValue>` variable; lambdas and method groups bind unchanged.

- `QuePaxaVersionedNode<TValue>`'s constructor refuses a committed record of another chain, which is the rule `FromState` already applied to a restored one. The public constructor was the fourth path a committed record can enter a host by and the only one that adopted the record's membership without comparing it: a host handed a foreign record ran that foreign chain, and then declined its own chain's records at the request, at the learn and at every later restore, so a deployment wiring error surfaced as a host that refused everything rather than as a refused construction. The comparison is the restore's, over the chain identity of the membership the record names against the genesis the host was handed, and it is made against the genesis rather than against the active membership because a host being constructed derives that membership from the very record under test, where a comparison would read one value twice and refuse nothing. A host given no record compares its genesis with itself and constructs as before. Nothing else about the membership is read: a record whose member list does not contain this host still constructs it, because that is how a joiner starts and how a replica a change removed keeps running until its deployment retires it. With this, the chain identity is checked on every path a committed record enters a host by — construction, restore, request and learn — and `ActiveConfiguration` names the genesis's chain in every state a host can reach. **Breaking** for a caller that constructed a host from a record of another chain: that call succeeded silently before and now throws `ArgumentException`.

- `QuePaxaVersionedNode<TValue>.Learn` refuses a record of another chain instead of adopting it, and `DeclinesLearn` is the classifier that refusal is raised from. Dissemination is the one path that adopts a record without being asked to serve anything, and it was the one path no chain identity was checked on: `Handle` declined another chain's request and `FromState` refused another chain's stored membership, while a publisher wired across two clusters could hand a host a record its chain never decided and that host would adopt it, move its live instance and derive the next leader from it. The comparison is the one `Handle` makes, over the chain identity the record's configuration names against the host's own, and both sites read one predicate so the rule cannot hold at a request and not at a learn. It is read before the version gate, because version order is a fact inside one chain and says nothing across two, so a foreign record standing below the held one is refused rather than reported as a record that did not advance. Nothing else about the membership is read there: a record that removes the host is still learned, because a removed host learns it is out from the record that removed it and a joiner catches up from whoever holds the record. `QuePaxaVersionedRunner<TValue>.LearnAsync` faults that call with the host's own refusal and keeps serving, through the classifier-gated filter the record path already uses, so a wiring defect at one publisher costs that call and never the host's loop, while the result still reports adoption alone and a record that merely did not advance still reports `false`. **Breaking** for a caller that handed a host a record of another chain: that call succeeded silently before and now throws `ArgumentException`.

- `QuePaxaVersionedRunner<TValue>` makes a learn durable when that learn installed a membership, whichever `LearnDurability` the caller named. The loop compares the host's `ActiveConfiguration` before the learn against the one it holds after, and runs the same durability gate a `Durable` learn runs when the two differ, so a record the host did not adopt installs nothing and owes nothing, and a run without a persist delegate keeps writing nothing at all. Eager persistence per disseminated record stays the demotion the durability knob exists to avoid, and this is the one exception worth its write: the record that installs a membership may be the only copy of it inside the membership it installs, so a host that adopted it in memory and crashed would come back serving under the membership the change replaced. The comparison is structural, over the chain identity and the ordered member list, because a membership that crossed a codec is the membership it was encoded from. `LearnAsync`'s result is unchanged and still reports adoption in memory alone.

- `QuePaxaVersionedRegister<TValue>` derives the recorder set it addresses instead of being handed one. The constructor takes the chain's genesis `QuePaxaConfiguration`, this replica, a hedging base delay and a `ResolveRecorderEndpointDelegate<TValue>` where it took an endpoint list and a `QuePaxaLeaderSchedule`, the catch-up readers become a `ResolveCommittedRecordReaderDelegate<TValue>`, and the construction-time check that this replica appears in the schedule is gone: a register for a replica the membership does not list is how a joiner starts and what a removed replica becomes, so membership is answered per version by a new `QuePaxaWriteStatus.OutsideConfiguration`, which is returned rather than thrown and spends no attempt, and `Delay` becomes `TimeSpan?` because a non-member has no position in the hedging order. Every attempt reads the committed record once into a `RegisterInstance` — version, membership, previous writer — before it waits its delay, and resolves the version, the leader and the endpoints from that capture alone, so a record learned while the attempt was parked cannot move the instance under it. The endpoint array holds exactly one slot per member and an unresolvable member keeps its slot as an always-faulting endpoint, because the quorum is the array's length and a dropped slot shrinks the majority rather than the reachability. `ReconfigureAsync` installs the membership a `ChangeConfigurationDelegate` computes while carrying the committed value forward, re-applying the delta against the winner when an attempt is superseded, refusing before anything is committed and writing nothing when the change is the identity; an ordinary write carries the membership forward unchanged and cannot move it. `ReadReadinessAsync` reports what each member has learned, over a new `ObserveMemberVersionDelegate`, as `RegisterReadiness` and `MemberReadiness`, which is the observable a membership change is gated on. `WriteAsync` takes the named `ComputeRegisterValueDelegate<TValue>` where it took a naked `Func<TValue?, TValue>`. **Breaking** for every caller that constructs a versioned register or writes through one.

- `VersionedRecordReply<TValue>` carries the host that produced it. The record gains a `ReplicaId Recorder` field in the second position, filled with the host's own identity, and the versioned reply's JSON encoding gains a required `recorder` slot beside `version`. A versioned register counts a quorum over distinct members of the membership it addressed and reaches them through an endpoint map a deployment wires by hand, so it now checks that the endpoint it aimed at one member was answered by that member, beside the existing check that the reply came from the instance it addressed: two slots pointing at one host would otherwise let one replica be counted twice and a decision be taken by a minority of the instance it names. The check is exact under crash faults and **is not authentication** — the answering host asserts its own name and nothing verifies it — so it catches a wiring error and nothing a host that lies would do. **Breaking** on the reply's shape and on the reply wire.

- `PublishCommittedRecordDelegate<TValue>` takes the audience the register computed. The delegate gains an `ImmutableArray<ReplicaId> audience` parameter in the second position, which is the union of the membership that decided the record and the membership it installs: for an ordinary decide the union degenerates to the deciding membership, and at a configuration boundary the incoming half hands a joiner the installing record rather than leaving it to learn the record by some other route, while the outgoing half hands a departing host the record that removed it. A publish fault no longer fails the write. The register guards the await and returns the decided outcome whatever the push does, cancellation included and the caller's own token included, because a caller told its committed write failed would retry a write that had already landed, and a boundary push addresses exactly the cold hosts most likely to fault; what reports a push that did not land is `ReadReadinessAsync` and never a write's result. **Breaking** for every implementation of the delegate.

- `QuePaxaVersionedNode<TValue>` derives the membership it runs under instead of being configured with a leader schedule. The constructor and `FromState` take the chain's genesis `QuePaxaConfiguration` and the host's own `ReplicaId` where they took a `QuePaxaLeaderSchedule`, and the host exposes `Genesis`, `Self`, `ActiveConfiguration` and a `LeaderSchedule` that is now a memo rather than a fixture: the active membership is the committed record's `NextConfiguration`, or genesis when no record has been learned, recomputed only where the record moves — the constructor, `Learn` and `FromState` — because a membership a host can be told is a membership two hosts can be told differently for one instance. `Declines` is new beside `Serves` and is the classifier the host's own refusals are raised from, so `Handle` and the classifier are one predicate consulted twice rather than two rules kept in step. Beyond the version bound it refuses a request whose carried record names another chain, one addressed to a host the active membership no longer contains, and one whose carried record disagrees with its envelope about its own version — the last of these being a defective proposer's request that would wedge the instance if it decided, since every host learning the decision refuses the mismatch before it can adopt the record. `QuePaxaVersionedRunner<TValue>`'s decline filter reads `Declines` where it read `!Serves(version)`, in the same change, or a chain or membership refusal would be classified as a defect and end the loop. **Breaking** for every caller that constructs or restores a versioned host.

- `QuePaxaVersionedNodeState<TValue>` gains a required, non-null `ActiveConfiguration` field in the fourth position, validated on construction and on a `with` expression, and the state's JSON encoding gains a required `activeConfiguration` slot between `configuredLeader` and `recorder`. It is stored rather than recomputed for the reason the stored leader and the stored version are: a restore that recomputed it from the committed record would compare it with itself, while a stored copy lets the restore refuse a register from one instance sitting beside a configuration from another, which is what a snapshot torn across a reconfiguration leaves behind. `FromState` gains that comparison and one more that is not a tear at all — the restored membership must name the chain the host's genesis names, so a store attached to the wrong cluster, or a genesis edited under a restarting host, refuses to start rather than joining a chain it never agreed with. Both new rules are evaluated after the leader and version cross-checks and before the step-zero rebuild, and all four read only the record and the genesis, never the stored copies they compare against, so one torn field trips exactly one rule. **Breaking** twice over: every `QuePaxaVersionedNodeState<TValue>` construction gains an argument, and the state encoding refuses payloads written before the slot existed.

- `VersionedValue<TValue>` carries the membership it was decided under. The record gains a required, non-null `QuePaxaConfiguration` field in the third position, `NextConfiguration`, validated on construction and on a `with` expression, and the record's JSON encoding gains a required `configuration` slot between `writer` and `value` carrying the chain identity and the ordered member list. A record or state payload missing the slot is refused by name rather than defaulted, because a membership silently defaulted in is exactly the reconfiguration hazard the membership arc exists to remove; the nested object's own `cluster` and `members` slots are required under their own label, and the decoder adds no rule of its own — every constraint stays with `QuePaxaConfiguration.Create`. `QuePaxaVersionedRegister<TValue>` stamps every record it constructs with one configuration derived from its schedule's agreed order, deterministic across replicas so all stamp the same value; the genesis constructor parameter and the committed carry-forward replace that derivation in the arc's later slices. **Breaking** twice over: every `VersionedValue<TValue>` construction gains an argument, and the record encoding refuses payloads written before the slot existed.

The `System.Security.Cryptography.Xml` transitive override rises to 10.0.11 and stays in place, because `SIL.ReleaseTasks` remains at 3.2.0 and still carries the vulnerable 9.0.0 the override exists to displace. `MSTest.Sdk` 4.3.3, `Microsoft.CodeAnalysis.BannedApiAnalyzers` 5.6.0, `Microsoft.Sbom.Targets` 4.1.5, `BenchmarkDotNet` 0.15.8 and `LiquidTestReports.Markdown` 2.0.0-beta.6 are unchanged, each already the latest release on its line. Of the CI action pins only `step-security/harden-runner` had a newer release and moves to v2.20.1 (from v2.20.0); `actions/checkout` v7.0.1, `actions/setup-dotnet` v6.0.0, `actions/upload-artifact` v7.0.1, `actions/download-artifact` v8.0.1, `EnricoMi/publish-unit-test-result-action` v2.24.0 and `NuGet/login` v1.2.0 were already at theirs.

- A `QuePaxaVersionedNode<TValue>` is owned by at most one runner at a time and now enforces it. `QuePaxaVersionedRunner<TValue>.RunAsync` claims the node for the life of its loop, and while it holds the claim the node's `Handle`, `Learn`, `MakeDurableAsync` and `ToState` throw `InvalidOperationException` rather than interleaving with the loop that is driving them; a second runner's `RunAsync` over a claimed node throws the same way. A `Handle` racing the loop replaces the recorder and a `Learn` replaces both the record and the instance, which are exactly the two references the durability gate compares, so a latch admitting either would enforce the least dangerous third of the invariant it exists for. `ToState` is guarded for a related reason rather than because it mutates anything: a snapshot is four fields read off those same two references, which a learn replaces in two separate stores, so a snapshot taken beside a running loop can pair a committed record with the register of another instance — the torn state `FromState` exists to refuse, produced by a reader rather than by a half-landed write. A host that wants a sequenced snapshot takes it inside its `PersistVersionedNodeDelegate<TValue>`, which the loop invokes while it owns the node. `Committed`, `LiveVersion`, `Serves` and `Recorder` stay open, because each answers from one read of one reference to an immutable value, so an off-loop reader sees a stale answer but never a torn one; `Serves` now reads the committed record once and derives both its arms from that one snapshot, so the documented ordering that keeps the classifier off the throwing path is structural rather than merely textual. The claim is released when the loop ends on any path and before `RunAsync`'s task completes, so the documented restart — a fresh runner over the same node after a failed write — is unaffected. This was the single-ownership floor the runner's remarks stated in prose. `MakeDurableAsync` also stops being `async`, so both its refusals now reach the call site rather than the awaiting continuation. **Breaking** for a host that snapshotted or mutated a node beside its own runner.

- `QuePaxaVersionedRunner<TValue>.LearnAsync` takes a `LearnDurability` naming how far the learn must get before the call completes: `InMemory` keeps the shipped behavior, where a learned record becomes durable with the first reply that depends on it or with a checkpoint, and `Durable` makes the host's state durable before the call completes, so catch-up learning is crash-safe in one call. The parameter is required rather than defaulted, because a learn's crash-safety is a decision the caller makes and a default would hide it at the call sites where getting it wrong is the hazard. Eager persistence is not the default: one write per disseminated record on every host is a demotion, and `Durable` is the per-call opt-in. The result still reports adoption in memory alone, because after a `Durable` call that completed the state IS durable and after one that faulted nothing is promised. An undefined value is refused rather than taking the in-memory arm, which is the fail-open direction on a durability knob. **Breaking** for every `LearnAsync` caller.

- `QuePaxaVersionedNode<TValue>` exposes its recorder rather than the recorder node holding it: `Node` is gone and `Recorder` takes its place. The removed property made a hazard expressible — a host could drive the inner node's own `RunAsync` with a bare `PersistRecorderDelegate<TValue>` and make durable a register that names no instance, which is the torn pairing `FromState` exists to refuse, written deliberately, while the reply left through the inner sink without the versioned envelope so the proposer's per-instance correlation check never ran. With the property gone the misuse is unstateable rather than guarded against, and the recorder — immutable, and what every reader of the old property actually wanted — is what the surface offers. `QuePaxaNode<TValue>` and its loop are unchanged and remain the surface for a bare, unversioned recorder node. **Breaking** for every reader of `Node`.

- The Raft surface carries `Term` and `LogIndex` where it carried bare `long`s: `RequestVoteRequest`, `RequestVoteReply`, `AppendEntriesRequest<TCommand>`, `AppendEntriesReply`, `RaftLogEntry<TCommand>`, `RaftNodeState<TCommand>`, `FollowerProgress`, `ApplyCommittedDelegate<TCommand>`, `RaftNode<TCommand>.CurrentTerm`, `.CommitIndex` and `.Propose`, and `RaftRunner<TCommand>.ProposeAsync`. Figure 2 places an index and a term side by side — a candidate's last log index beside its last log term, a leader's previous log index beside that entry's term — and two adjacent parameters of one primitive type exchange without a diagnostic while changing which rule the receiver applies. The types also hold the invariants the documentation had only stated in prose: a term is non-negative, an index is non-negative with zero the empty prefix ahead of the first entry, and the 1-based-to-0-based conversion happens in one place, `LogIndex.Position`, which refuses the empty prefix rather than addressing the element below the store. **Breaking** for every caller that constructs or reads these members.

- Both Raft value types are capped one below two to the fifty-third, the bound `RegisterVersion` takes and for the same reason: the field crosses JSON as a bare number, and every integer up to that bound is exactly representable as an IEEE double while nothing above it is, so two terms above it reach a double-parsing consumer as one value. What that costs is specific rather than cosmetic, because every term rule in Figure 2 is a comparison: two terms that arrive equal let a stale request pass the staleness test and let a receiver skip the step-down a higher term requires, and for an index the same collapse makes the consistency check match the wrong entry. The bound costs a deployment nothing, since reaching it takes two to the fifty-third elections or log entries. `Term.Next()` and `LogIndex.Next()` throw at `MaxValue` rather than incrementing into the range validator, whose message would name the value instead of the exhaustion that happened. **Breaking** for a peer that sent a term or index above the bound; it now fails closed as `MessageDeserializationException`.

- `RaftLogEntry<TCommand>` refuses a term below `Term.First`, so a log entry tagged with the term a node holds before any election is unrepresentable rather than refused downstream. Only an elected leader creates an entry, and the rule previously existed twice, in `RaftJson` and in `RaftNode.FromState`, with nothing enforcing it for an entry built in process; both copies are gone. `FromState` now checks only what a single value cannot be wrong about on its own — a vote's width and membership, log terms that decrease, and a last log term above the current term.

- `ChangeOutcome<TValue>` gains a third positional member, `AcceptedCount`, reporting how many acceptors accepted the change. It is zero when the attempt failed before any accept was sent, and reports the true count when accepts were sent but fell short of a quorum. **Breaking** for a caller that constructs the record positionally, deconstructs it positionally, or matches it with a positional pattern, because the synthesized `Deconstruct` arity follows the parameter list; property access is unaffected. `FastProposer.RecoverAsync` previously had no way to report the breadth its own piggyback rule depends on: a next fast ballot may only be armed when the accept carrying it reaches at least the fast quorum, because a round armed on fewer is one no fast quorum can complete, so every following fast write falls back. `TryFastWriteAsync` already reported this and `RecoverAsync` now does too, which makes the recovery-to-fast handoff expressible.

- `CborChannelSerialization.CreateDeserializer` now refuses a payload carrying data after the message instead of decoding the message and ignoring the surplus. Allowing trailing bytes let several distinct byte sequences decode to one message, which contradicts the deterministic canonical encoding the class exists to provide and which the JSON channel already refuses trailing data to keep. Frames carry their own length, so anything past the message is slack a sender chose to add. **Breaking** for a sender that padded its frames; the surplus now surfaces as `MessageDeserializationException` like every other malformed payload.

### Added

- The dynamic fleet becomes observable first-class. `ReadReadinessAsync` gains an overload taking the membership to measure over, so the incoming side of a change is gated before any register runs under it and a post-outage sweep can ask over whatever membership a surviving record names; a membership of another chain is refused, because a report over it would answer a question about a different register. `QuePaxaConfiguration` gains `Joining` and `Leaving`, the delta an operator acts on at a boundary — who an admission disseminates to first, who a readiness gate must stop counting — refused across chains because two chains never were one fleet. `QuePaxaVersionedNode<TValue>` gains `Instance`, the version, membership and previous writer derived from one capture of the committed record, so an off-loop reader cannot pair a new record with an old membership. `QuePaxaVersionedRunner<TValue>` exposes `Node`, whose documented off-loop reads are what a wire host serves an operations endpoint from; every mutating member still throws under the loop's ownership claim, so the export widens what a host can read and nothing it can interleave.

- `ReceiveCommittedRecordDelegate<TValue>` names the dissemination receive leg: offer a committed record to one host under the sender's named `LearnDurability`, completing when the learn has got that far. `QuePaxaVersionedRunner<TValue>.LearnAsync` is one by method-group conversion and its remarks now say so, the way `RecordAsync` and `ReadCommittedAsync` already name their delegates, so a socketed consumer assigns the runner's own method instead of minting the delegate itself. `PublishCommittedRecordDelegate<TValue>`'s remarks name it as the shape an offer invokes per audience host and settle what each receiving host owes — the runner's learn where a runner owns the host, the node's learn wrapped where none does, with a co-located register free to take the same record through its own learn and owing nothing, because a register that was not told catches up through its own read and its own superseded attempts. The socket suite now disseminates through it over the wire: a scenario's publish crosses loopback TCP into each host's own learn, which is the receive leg every socketed deployment wires.

- `ClusterId` and `QuePaxaConfiguration` open the dynamic-membership surface. A configuration is a validated, order-sensitive member list with a majority quorum, `With`/`Without` producing the next membership without re-minting identity, and hand-written equality that is element-wise over the members and refuses to equate configurations from different chains. A `ClusterId` is the 32-byte chain identity minted once at genesis by an order-sensitive SHA-256 digest over a domain-separated, count-prefixed encoding of the founding member list; the encoding is a permanent contract — a build that changed it would mint new identities for existing chains — and is pinned by a known-answer vector. Duplicate members are refused at both `Create` and `CreateGenesis`, because a member listed twice answers twice and counts twice toward a quorum, which breaks quorum injectivity at exactly the configuration an operator hand-writes; an empty list is refused, and `Without` refuses to empty a configuration. Nothing consumes the types yet: the record's configuration field, the host's enforcement and the reconfiguration surface follow in the membership arc's later slices.

- `QuePaxaVersionedRunner<TValue>.ReadCommittedAsync` answers a catch-up read through the runner's own queue, making the host's state durable before it reports the record. There was no implementation of `ReadCommittedRecordDelegate<TValue>` in the library at all, so every host that wired one had to go around the loop: an unsequenced read of state the loop is writing, and a republish of a record the host has learned and not yet persisted. A replica catching up adopts what a host reports and moves to the next version on it, so that host could lose the record in a crash while a peer had already built on it; the read now persists first, and the gate makes that free whenever the host owes no write — which is every host that has answered a request since its last learn, while a host that owes one owes it because it learned and has not replied since or because a write failed, the two states where republishing is the hazard. It carries no durability knob deliberately, both because a knob would break the method-group conversion and because the whole point of the adoption is that a republish is not permitted to be unpersisted: it IS a `ReadCommittedRecordDelegate<TValue>`, so a host assigns it where `QuePaxaVersionedRegister<TValue>` expects one, and it is also how a host builds a sequenced `ObserveCommittedVersionDelegate` without a second unsequenced reader. A read on a host that learned its way to `RegisterVersion.MaxValue` without an intervening write ends the loop through the snapshot's documented throw, which is the same terminal-by-design contract the checkpoint carries.

- A Fast CASPaxos acceptor can be rebuilt from durable state: `FastAcceptorState<TValue>` carries the promise, the accepted ballot and the accepted value, `FastAcceptor<TValue>.ToState` snapshots them, `FastAcceptor<TValue>.FromState` validates them back into an acceptor, and `ConsensusNode<TValue>` gains a constructor taking the restored acceptor and treating it as already durable, so the durability gate owes no write for the state the host restored and the first write of a run is for the first request that changes it. Persisting before replying was already the node's obligation, but nothing could turn the persisted bytes back into an acceptor, so the delegate documented a hazard the library gave no way to avoid. The restore takes no configuration beside the state, unlike the recorder's leader and Raft's identity and membership, because an acceptor carries none — and for the same reason it carries no replica identity, so restoring one replica's snapshot onto another passes every rule and makes two acceptors report an accept only one of them made, which stays the host's filing obligation. It refuses fail-closed every state no acceptor can hold: a promise below the initial fast ballot, an accepted ballot that is neither the zero ballot nor at least the initial fast ballot, an accepted ballot above the promise, and a value under the zero accepted ballot. Those four are read off `Prepare` and `Accept` and are exact in both directions: the promise starts pre-promised at the initial fast ballot and only ever rises, the accepted ballot is written only beside the value and only by an accept whose ballot stood at or above the promise, and every other combination is one a sequence of prepares and accepts reaches — including a fast promise above the initial round with nothing accepted, a fast accepted round above the first, a fast accepted value under a later classic promise, and the type's default value at a real accepted ballot, none of which the restore may refuse. Two of the rules are range checks over a single slot, which would normally belong to a value constructor, because `FastBallot`'s constructor is public and unvalidated by necessity: the zero ballot is its default and it is the ballot the wire carries. `ToState` and `FromState` are inverses everywhere including the initial acceptor, an asymmetry with `QuePaxaRecorder<TValue>`, whose unwritten state the restore refuses: a recorder returning at step zero takes a fresh first proposal at a step a proposer has already read, while an acceptor returning at its initial state is pre-promised exactly as a node that lost everything is, so refusing it would refuse the state `new ConsensusNode<TValue>()` itself stands in. `PersistAcceptorDelegate<TValue>` now names the restore and the write's shape: the three fields are one durable write and not three, because a store that lands a subset can leave a mix of two faithful snapshots that passes every rule the restore owns and still contradicts a reply already sent — the accepted ballot of the newer snapshot beside the accepted value of the older one makes the acceptor answer a later prepare with a different value under a ballot a proposer was already told carried another — so no relational rule substitutes for the write landing whole or the stored document being self-checking. `ConsensusMessageJson` gains `CreateAcceptorStateSerializer` and `CreateAcceptorStateDeserializer`, which encode the accepted ballot and the value exactly as a prepare reply encodes its matching fields while the promise is a ballot here where a reply's `promised` is a boolean, refuse only what the encoding can be wrong about, and hand every ballot to the same unvalidated reader the wire uses, so a negative round and a round-zero ballot owning a proposer both decode and are refused by `FromState` as the single place that owns every rule, the single-slot range checks included. The parameterless `ConsensusNode<TValue>` constructor stays and seeds through the same path over the initial acceptor, because an acceptor carries no configuration a defaulted node could get wrong.

- The versioned recorder host gains its durability sequencing and its run loop: `PersistVersionedNodeDelegate<TValue>`, `QuePaxaVersionedNode<TValue>.MakeDurableAsync` and `.Serves`, and `QuePaxaVersionedRunner<TValue>`. The delegate receives the immutable `QuePaxaVersionedNodeState<TValue>` — the exact value `FromState` restores, snapshotted only when a write is owed — because the host is mutable and a store that read it after an await could make durable a state the gate then marks as written. `MakeDurableAsync` gates on what was last made durable, held on the node as two references compared by reference and advanced only after the write returns, so a throwing store leaves the host owing exactly the write it owed and a redelivery after a failed write retries the write before it answers; the baseline is node state rather than loop state because a host restarts by building a fresh runner over the same node. The rule is the or of the two arms and the arms are independent: a request that changes nothing costs no write once its state is durable, and a learn usually moves both references but a host learning one leaderless-instance record after another keeps the shared leaderless recorder singleton across the learn, where the committed arm is the only one that fires — reachable only through a checkpoint, since every request's step sits above zero and the first request after any learn replaces the register. `QuePaxaVersionedRunner<TValue>` drives one host over a single-consumer work queue in the `RaftRunner` shape: `RecordAsync` is a `VersionedRecorderEndpointDelegate<VersionedValue<TValue>>` by method-group conversion and answers on its own completion, `LearnAsync` queues dissemination beside the requests and reports adoption in memory — applied, not durable — and `MakeDurableAsync` sequences a checkpoint through the same queue. A declined instance faults that call alone with the exception the host threw, unwrapped, while the loop keeps serving: the filter reads `Serves`, a total classifier that never throws and reports false for every version on a spent host, so both documented refusals — the version mismatch and the spent-range throw — stay per-call and any other throw is a defect that ends the loop. A wire host owes the opaque reduction of that fault, because the exception names the live version only in prose and no protocol field carries it. A persist failure ends the loop, discards the computed reply on every path, and faults every unanswered call with the loop failure as inner exception, since a proposer's attempt budget acts on faults and hangs on silence; a call cancelled by its own token completes cancelled while its queued work still runs, and `RunAsync` runs once, so a restart after a failure takes a fresh runner over the same node.

- A QuePaxa versioned recorder host can be rebuilt from durable state: `QuePaxaVersionedNodeState<TValue>` carries the committed record the host has learned, the version its recorder serves, the leader that recorder enforces and the recorder's own state, with `QuePaxaVersionedNode<TValue>.ToState` and `.FromState` as the pair. Only the recorder was restorable before, while the committed record every recorder derives its leader from had no persistence at all, so a host that restarted came back serving an instance that had already decided, with an empty register, and the register that was restorable was the one the host had least trouble rebuilding. Two of the four fields are derivable from the committed record and are stored anyway, which is what makes the restore's cross-checks able to fail: a restore that recomputed the leader and the version would compare each with itself, while a stored copy lets it compare what a host wrote against what its own record implies. So a snapshot whose stored leader is not the one the schedule derives from the restored record is refused, because two hosts holding records that imply different leaders for one instance admit two reserved claims at the step the fast path reads, and a host whose own snapshot says so refuses to start rather than joining as the second leader; and a snapshot whose stored version is not the one after the restored record's is refused, because a register from one instance beside a record from another is a snapshot written in two parts and torn between them. The four fields are therefore one durable write and not two, and they must be stable before any reply that depends on them leaves the process, which is the obligation `PersistRecorderDelegate<TValue>` already sequences one layer down reaching the committed record for the same reason: the leader is derived from it. A stale record that is internally consistent restores without complaint, which is correct rather than a gap, because the leader is a deterministic function of the record and the version gate keeps such a host off the live instance, so falling behind costs availability and never agreement. Unlike the recorder's own pair, this one is an inverse over every state a host reaches, including the interval between learning a version and answering the first request for the next one, where the recorder stands at step zero: a host occupies that interval for as long as its key is idle and refusing to restart there would be a defect rather than a defence, while nothing was answered from a register at that step because a recorder records nothing below round one phase zero. The short circuit is the unwritten register exactly, so a step-zero snapshot carrying a proposal in any slot is refused rather than silently rebuilt, and every other step below the recorder's floor still reaches `QuePaxaRecorder<TValue>.FromState` and is refused there. A durable store that came back empty stays undetectable from these four fields and no field could make it so, since a wiped snapshot and a host that has genuinely learned nothing carry the same values. `QuePaxaMessageJson` gains `CreateVersionedNodeStateSerializer` and `CreateVersionedNodeStateDeserializer`, which refuse a missing field alone and leave both cross-checks to `FromState`, and the recorder nested inside a snapshot is encoded exactly as a standalone one is, so there is one encoding of a recorder state and not two.

- A QuePaxa recorder can be rebuilt from durable state: `QuePaxaRecorderState<TValue>` carries the four fields of `IntervalSummaryRegister<TValue>`, `QuePaxaRecorder<TValue>.ToState` snapshots them, and `QuePaxaRecorder<TValue>.FromState` validates them back into a recorder under the configured leader a deployment supplies beside them, because that leader is configuration derived from committed state rather than durable protocol state. Persisting before replying was already the node's obligation, but nothing could turn the persisted bytes back into a recorder, so the delegate documented a hazard the library gave no way to avoid. That prose previously named only the step and the first proposal as the durable set, so a host built against it is refused where it also dropped the current aggregate and restores without complaint where it persisted those three and dropped `PriorAggregate` above the round's first step, because a null prior aggregate is what a skipped step legitimately leaves and the restore cannot tell that apart from a field a host never wrote. The delegate also names the write's shape: the four fields are one durable write and not four, because a store that lands a subset of them can leave a mix of two faithful snapshots that is itself a state a recorder-driven register can hold, so it passes every rule the restore owns while contradicting a reply already sent, and no relational rule can substitute for the write landing whole or the stored document being self-checking. The restore refuses fail-closed every state no recorder-driven register can hold: a step below round one phase zero, a step above zero carrying no first proposal, a reserved priority at the round's first step owned by a lane other than the configured leader in either the first proposal or the current aggregate, a first proposal with no current aggregate, a current aggregate ordering below the first proposal at the same step, a prior aggregate at the round's first step, and a prior aggregate one step above it holding a reserved priority owned by a lane other than the configured leader, which is how far down the downgraded stream the carry reaches. Every one of those rests on the recorder's step floor rather than on what the register alone can produce — recorded at step zero directly the register will fold an aggregate while its first proposal stays null, which Algorithm 3 permits — so the register's own restore stays internal and unvalidated while this one is public and validated, and the obligation to reject a corrupt snapshot now has an owner rather than being left to whatever layer restores one. `ToState` and `FromState` are deliberately not inverses at the bottom of the range: an unwritten recorder snapshots at step zero and the restore refuses that snapshot, because a recorder returning at step zero takes a fresh first proposal at a step a proposer has already read, which is the crash the restore exists to prevent, while snapshotting has to succeed there because a host that persists unconditionally after every handled item would otherwise throw before it had recorded anything. `QuePaxaMessageJson` gains `CreateRecorderStateSerializer` and `CreateRecorderStateDeserializer`, which refuse only what the encoding can be wrong about and hand every value to its domain constructor, leaving every relational rule to `FromState` as the single place that owns them. `QuePaxaLeaderSchedule.RecorderFor` gains a restore overload taking a `QuePaxaRecorderState<TValue>`, so a restarting host derives the configured leader from the record it has learned through the same expression the live path uses rather than hand-wiring a lane, because a lane that does not match the derivation restores silently and puts two leaders on one instance.

- QuePaxa becomes a versioned register: `RegisterVersion`, `VersionedValue<TValue>`, `QuePaxaLeaderSchedule`, `QuePaxaVersionedNode<TValue>`, `QuePaxaVersionedRegister<TValue>`, `QuePaxaWriteStatus`, `QuePaxaWriteOutcome<TValue>`, `VersionedRecordRequest<TValue>`, `VersionedRecordReply<TValue>`, `VersionedRecorderEndpointDelegate<TValue>`, `ObserveCommittedVersionDelegate`, `ReadCommittedRecordDelegate<TValue>`, and `PublishCommittedRecordDelegate<TValue>`. Every write is a fresh consensus instance at the next version, and the version identifies the instance rather than a separate identifier being carried beside it. `RegisterVersion` is capped at one below two to the fifty-third rather than at the unsigned range, because a version crosses JSON as a bare number and every integer up to that bound survives an IEEE double exactly, so the demotion hazard `ProposalPriority` has to document does not arise for this field; the cost is that no value can detect a double-parsing reader, and the codec pins its accessor with a non-integral token instead.

- The reserved-priority defence gets its input from `QuePaxaLeaderSchedule`, which derives the next instance's leader from the previous version's writer over an agreed `HedgingSchedule`. The writer is a field of `VersionedValue<TValue>` rather than being read off the winning proposal's owner, because consensus agrees on the decided value and the decision drops the winning proposal's owner, so that the owner is nonetheless unique would rest on `ProposalKey`'s uniqueness contract, which nothing in the core enforces. One rotated schedule answers two questions that must not be confused: the leader it names is a safety input every recorder serving the instance must agree on, while every position's delay is hedging and gates nothing. A writer whose replica the order no longer contains yields a leaderless instance at every replica rather than throwing, and that is safe because it is uniform.

- `QuePaxaVersionedNode<TValue>` serves exactly one consensus instance, the version after the record it has learned, and declines every other by throwing. It cannot serve a window: a host that has learned version v can derive the leader for v+1 and for nothing else, and two hosts at different learned versions serving one instance would configure it with two different leaders. Recorders that derive different leaders violate agreement, because two reserved claims are then admitted at the step the fast path reads and the lemma that path rests on loses its hypothesis. A host that cannot derive the leader declines the instance rather than serving it leaderless: a recorder that declines a reserved claim records it at the lowest ordinary priority rather than dropping it, and does so at the round's first step, so serving leaderless among led recorders puts the leader's one logical proposal under two keys exactly where the fast path is read, and no quorum through that recorder can take it. Declining is a host act and not a protocol refusal: `RecordReply<TValue>` gains no rejection field, the fault reaches a proposer as an unreachable recorder, and the protocol keeps the refusal path it has never had. What this costs a deployment is that no write at v+1 can gather a quorum until a quorum of hosts has learned v, which is why `PublishCommittedRecordDelegate<TValue>` exists and why the record and the next version's first request may travel together.

- `QuePaxaVersionedRegister<TValue>` reports three outcomes rather than two, because an undecided attempt is not a lost one: its own proposal may still be carried by another proposer and decided later, so `QuePaxaWriteStatus` separates `Undecided` from `Superseded` and `WriteAsync` retries them differently — a superseded attempt adopts the winner, advances a version and recomputes, while an undecided one retries at the same version on a fresh lane against the same value. The commit test is over the whole decided record and not over the winning proposal's owner, mirroring `CheckpointedSequence.Seal`: a value can be chosen by a quorum and still not be this writer's. `WriteAsync` applies its update outside the round, once per attempt, which is the whole difference from `CasPaxosRegister.Change` applying one inside it, and it is why QuePaxa and CASPaxos are interchangeable only for updates that are idempotent, monotone, or abort-on-lose. The register is single-flight and refuses a concurrent write, because two writes at one version would propose on one lane and one proposal key naming two values is what the key's uniqueness contract forbids. `QuePaxaWriteOutcome<TValue>` carries the decision step, so `TookFastPath` reports the one-round-trip commit that is the register's whole quantitative claim, and `Activated`, so a hedged writer that stood down is distinguishable from one that ran and decided nothing.

- `QuePaxaMessageJson` gains the versioned envelope and the decided record: `CreateVersionedRequestSerializer`, `CreateVersionedRequestDeserializer`, `CreateVersionedReplySerializer`, `CreateVersionedReplyDeserializer`, `CreateVersionedValueWriter`, and `CreateVersionedValueReader`. The envelope wraps rather than extends, so `RecordRequest<TValue>` and `RecordReply<TValue>` keep their field sets and their byte-for-byte encodings, and the family still has no discriminator: a channel carrying the wrapper is monotyped exactly as one carrying the bare message, and the version is a guard that lets a host refuse a request meant for another instance. The record's own encoding lives in the library rather than in each consumer, because its writer field is what the next instance's leader is derived from; only the application value inside it stays caller-supplied.

- The QuePaxa message family gains JSON codecs: `QuePaxaMessageJson` builds serializers and deserializers for `RecordRequest<TValue>` and `RecordReply<TValue>`, with the caller supplying how to read and write `TValue`. There is no discriminator field, because QuePaxa has one message in each direction rather than a family to discriminate, and a decoded value re-runs every domain validator and gains none of its own: a step below round one phase zero, an absent priority on a request, a negative lane, and an identity of the wrong width all fail closed as `MessageDeserializationException`. A reserved priority above round one phase zero, the all-zero identity at lane zero, and the last representable step are all legal and round-trip unchanged, because each is a value the protocol depends on and a codec that refused it would stall a round rather than reject a message.

- `ProposalPriority` crosses the JSON wire as a bare number, and `QuePaxaMessageJson` documents what that costs. The reserved priority is `ulong.MaxValue`, which is above two to the fifty-third, so it and the largest ordinary priority are the same IEEE double: a consumer that parses JSON numbers as doubles cannot tell them apart, and a demoted reserved priority costs the round-one leader its fast path. The codec itself is exact, reading the field as an unsigned 64-bit integer and refusing a token carrying a fraction or an exponent, so the hazard belongs to a foreign reader rather than to this decoder. A deployment that carries these messages through a non-.NET JSON reader needs an exact encoding, and this codec is not one.

- A QuePaxa consensus core arrives as a transport-free safety core alongside the Fast CASPaxos one, with its own vocabulary throughout: `ProposerLane`, `ProposalPriority`, `ProposalKey`, `PrioritizedProposal<TValue>`, `ProposalPrioritySourceDelegate`, `RecorderStep`, `RecordSummary<TValue>`, `RecorderAnswer<TValue>`, `IntervalSummaryRegister<TValue>`, `QuePaxaRecorder<TValue>`, `QuePaxaRound<TValue>`, `QuePaxaStepKind`, `QuePaxaStepOutcome<TValue>`, `QuePaxaOutcome<TValue>`, and `QuePaxaRegister<TValue>`. `IntervalSummaryRegister<TValue>` is the constant-space interval summary register, keeping the step, the step's first proposal, the aggregate over the step and the aggregate over the step before it, with a skipped step clearing the prior aggregate. `RecorderStep` is the threshold logical clock, four times the round plus the phase, bounded at 256 complete rounds because a step arrives from the network once the core is wrapped in a transport and an unbounded clock is an attacker-controlled field. `QuePaxaRound<TValue>` carries the protocol rules themselves: `RedrawsPriority` and `NextSend` say what one send to one recorder carries, and `Conclude` says what a majority of answers means, deciding on the leader fast path at the first step or on the phase-two comparison two steps after that. `QuePaxaRegister<TValue>` is the synchronous driver over those rules, stepping one proposer against a chosen set of recorders. The quorum is a strict majority and `Conclude` derives it from the recorder count rather than accepting one, because a sub-majority answer set need not intersect another proposer's and the whole agreement argument is that two majorities intersect.

- QuePaxa and Fast CASPaxos are not drop-in equivalents, and the surface says so rather than letting the substitution look free. CASPaxos applies a change function to the value recovered inside the round, so a caller's intent survives contention; QuePaxa decides among proposed values, so a proposer that loses must re-read and re-propose. `QuePaxaOutcome<TValue>` therefore reports `DecidedBy`, the owner of the decided proposal rather than the proposer that observed the decision, which is what a caller reads to learn that someone else's value won. The two are interchangeable only for updates that are idempotent, monotone, or explicitly abort-on-lose. `ChangeOutcome<TValue>`, `FastBallot`, and `ConsensusEndpointDelegate` remain the Fast CASPaxos vocabulary and are not shared with QuePaxa, which carries its own outcome, ballot-free step, and endpoint delegate throughout.

- The round-one leader holds a reserved top priority, and `QuePaxaRecorder<TValue>` is where that reservation is enforced: a reserved-priority claim is honoured only from the instance's configured leader and is otherwise recorded at the lowest ordinary priority, so the round proceeds through the ordinary phases instead of losing the proposal. The rule applies at the round's first step alone and a reserved priority arriving above it is recorded verbatim, because the priority earns a decision only where the fast path reads it: rewriting it higher up defends nothing and gives one logical proposal a second key at every recorder that declines it, which is a proposal its neighbours do not hold. Recorder-side enforcement is the load-bearing rule rather than agreement among proposers, because a proposer that wrongly believes it leads cannot be prevented from believing it, so the claim has to be denied where it is recorded rather than trusted where it is made. Recorders that honour different leaders reproduce the divergence hazard. `PrioritizedProposal<TValue>.WithPriority` re-prioritizes without restamping the owner, so a proposal carried across a round boundary keeps the identity of the proposer that originated it. Comparisons on the fast path and at the phase-two decision are over whole proposals rather than proposal keys, so a proposer never decides a value other than the one the gathered record carries; `TValue` must therefore carry value equality. Keeping one proposal key to one value inside an instance remains the caller's obligation, and `ProposerLane` carries a lane so that a replica with two concurrent proposals can keep it.

- The QuePaxa core gains its message-driven layer: `RecordRequest<TValue>`, `RecordReply<TValue>`, `RecorderEndpointDelegate<TValue>`, `SendRecordReplyDelegate<TValue>`, `PersistRecorderDelegate<TValue>`, `QuePaxaNode<TValue>`, and `QuePaxaProposer<TValue>`. `QuePaxaNode<TValue>` is the stateful recorder in the `ConsensusNode<TValue>` idiom, holding one instance and, wherever a `PersistRecorderDelegate<TValue>` is supplied, persisting before it replies, and it is constructed from the recorder it serves rather than defaulting, because a recorder carries the configured leader and a leaderless default would silently downgrade every reserved claim. `QuePaxaProposer<TValue>` runs the four phases over the endpoints and acts on the first quorum to answer rather than waiting for every recorder, which is quorum latency rather than total latency and is what a majority-sized quorum buys over a supermajority. There is no rejection field on a reply: every request is served, and a reserved-priority claim from a proposer that is not the configured leader is recorded at the lowest ordinary priority at the round's first step so the round proceeds through the ordinary phases.

- A QuePaxa request may be re-delivered to a recorder that did not answer, and re-delivery must be identical. Each step draws its per-recorder proposals once and retains the requests for that step, so a recorder that faulted is asked again with the same step, proposal and priority; a second identical record leaves a recorder's state untouched, while a fresh draw would put two distinct proposal keys at one recorder and step. `IntervalSummaryRegister<TValue>.Record` and `QuePaxaRecorder<TValue>.Record` return the very instance they were called on whenever nothing would have changed, which covers both a record below the current step and an idempotent fold at the current step, so a retransmission whose state has been made durable costs no further durability write. `QuePaxaProposer<TValue>` proposes once and a second call throws: re-proposing on one lane would redraw at the first step, and the recovery is a fresh `ProposerLane` on a fresh proposer, which is a distinct proposer identity rather than a repeat of the same one. A recorder abandoned at one step is asked again at the next with a full attempt budget.

- `HedgingSchedule`, `HedgedFastWriter<TValue>`, `HedgedFastWriteOutcome`, and `FastRoundProgressDelegate` put Fast CASPaxos's leaderless fast round on a delayed-activation schedule. The schedule is an agreed replica order and a base delay; the replica first in the order writes immediately and each later replica waits its position times the base delay, optionally standing down if the host reports that the round was already driven. Nothing in the protocol changes: ballots, quorum rules, and acceptor state are untouched, and the delay decides only when a writer sends, so a delay of zero reproduces the unhedged behaviour exactly. `HedgedFastWriter<TValue>` holds no clock and runs its delay against an injected `TimeProvider`. The order must be an agreed fact — a configured order, a committed decision, or `RotateTo` over the previously committed writer — because replicas that rank the candidates differently each believe they are first and write concurrently.

### Fixed

- `QuePaxaVersionedRegister<TValue>`'s write documentation promised an `InvalidOperationException` for a recorder that answered for another instance or named another member, which is not what happens: those replies are refused at the counting site and absorbed as that recorder's unavailability, so they surface as an undecided outcome rather than a throw. The documented throw is now the one the register actually makes — a round that decided a record carrying a version other than the instance's own, a misrouted decision refused before adoption because a decision let into the committed state would set the next instance's leader — and that arm is pinned by a vector whose transport keeps every envelope honest while rewriting the records inside the gathered proposals.

- `MessageChannelReader<TMessage>`, `OwnedMessageChannelReader<TMessage>` and `ItemStreamChannelReader<TItem>` gain `CancelPendingRead` and end gracefully on a canceled read, the message readers ending their enumeration and the item reader completing its read task. The pipe's own stop surface was unreachable through the codec: the read loop never consulted `ReadResult.IsCanceled`, so it swallowed the canceled read and issued another one, and a consumer stopping mid-stream had no way to complete a call already in flight. A canceled read now ends the stream as a writer's completion does, except that no partial-frame check runs, because the consumer ended it rather than the peer. The canceled read itself delivers nothing, and rents nothing either, an owned value renting only at deserialization the canceled arm never reaches; frames already taken from the pipe into the buffer in hand are still delivered until that buffer is exhausted, so the stop takes effect at the next read from the pipe and a consumer that must not observe further messages stops consuming rather than relying on the cancel. Cancelling with no read pending cancels the next one, which is `PipeReader`'s own contract, and cancelling a stream that already ended is tolerated, so a teardown may cancel unconditionally. That is what the QuePaxa socket tests' teardown now does: a proposer abandons an outstanding endpoint call by design and the abandoned call sits in its reply enumerator's `MoveNextAsync`, where disposing the enumerator threw `NotSupportedException` about one full-suite run in eight; teardown cancels every reader, takes every connection's gate as the barrier proving no call is in flight, and only then disposes the enumerators.

- `QuePaxaVersionedRegister<TValue>.ReadAsync` skips a host whose runner has stopped instead of abandoning the whole catch-up round on it. The round rethrew every `OperationCanceledException` a host answered with, on the premise that a cancellation is the caller's own signal arriving by another route. That premise stopped holding once a runner answered its pending calls cancelled under its own token rather than faulting them, which is the contract that lets a caller tell a stopped host from a refusing one: a single stopped host then aborted the read at every host after it, so a caller whose own token was never signalled learned nothing from any of the survivors, and a host whose runner had merely completed was skipped while one that was cancelled was not — two shutdown paths disagreeing about the same state. The rethrow is now guarded on the caller's own token actually being signalled, so a cancellation that is not the caller's is that host's unavailability wearing a cancellation's type and is skipped like any other failing host, while the caller's own signal still ends the round rather than handing back an answer assembled from the hosts after it.

- `ConsensusNode<TValue>.RunAsync` gates its durable write on what was last persisted rather than on whether the request it is serving changed the acceptor, taking the form `QuePaxaNode<TValue>.RunAsync` already carries. The two gates come apart wherever the acceptor has moved past what was last made durable without the current request changing it, and the path that made the old gate a defect is the retransmission the idempotent accept retry makes ordinary: a request advances the acceptor, the durable write throws and the reply is correctly withheld, the host restarts the loop on the same node, the proposer re-delivers the identical request, and the retry answers from the same instance — so a gate that asked whether this request changed the state skipped the write and sent a reply announcing an accept that never reached the disk, on exactly the retransmission that makes persist-before-reply matter. The same comparison now also makes the first gated reply durable after requests handled through `Handle` or a run without a delegate, which costs one write and closes the same window. On the steady path a rejected request still costs no write: once a write has succeeded, the acceptor and the last persisted state stay the same object until the next change.

- `RaftNode<TCommand>` now filters every identity that arrives from the wire against the configured membership, which only the commit tally and the durable-state restore did before. `ReceiveVote` discards a reply from a non-member instead of tallying it, so a vote from outside the cluster can no longer complete an election no majority of the membership granted. `HandleRequestVote` refuses a vote to a non-member candidate, which `FromState` already refused to restore, so the node can no longer reach a state its own restore path rejects. `HandleAppendEntries` refuses entries naming a non-member leader, which would otherwise replicate a log no quorum agreed on. `ReceiveAppendEntriesReply` discards a non-member reply. Each filter runs before the term rule rather than after it, so a replica outside the membership cannot raise a member's term either; discarding it only at the tally leaves a stranger able to unseat a leader the cluster agreed on without ever completing a quorum, which is the weaker lever and the one a tally-only filter keeps open.

- `RaftNode<TCommand>` addresses its per-follower `nextIndex` and `matchIndex` by position in the membership rather than keying them by identity, so a replica outside the membership has no slot and cannot be recorded at all. This replaces a runtime check with a representation: the commit tally reads the arrays directly instead of hashing an identity per member per candidate index, and the leader can no longer accumulate an entry per identity a peer supplies.

- `FastCasPaxosRegister<TValue>.ProposeFastReaching` now refuses a repeated acceptor index instead of folding it into the count. An acceptor reached twice answers twice, because the second accept is an idempotent retry of the same ballot and value and so succeeds, and the returned count is what a caller compares against `FastQuorum`; three acceptors could therefore report a quorum of four and a value never accepted by a fast quorum would be treated as committed. The method already validated the other half of the same parameter. **Breaking** for a caller that passed a list with repeats.

- `QuePaxaVersionedRegister<TValue>` allocates the proposer lane per proposal rather than per call, so a caller cannot put two values under one proposal key by calling `TryWriteAsync` again at a version it left undecided. For the version's leader the key is the reserved priority on lane zero and so is identical whatever the value, which made reuse a silent divergence; the lane counter is keyed on the version, so a version the register has not proposed at starts again at lane zero and the reserved priority stays reachable.

The translation-map merge is no longer last-writer-wins: a dropped dot resolves to the maximum-counter merged vertex reachable through the union of both maps, making the merge commutative across states compacted at different frontiers. Retention classification is now an explicit-stack post-order walk, so long stable deleted runs compact without call-stack growth.

- The public `ReconciliationContract` constructor now enforces a checksum-width production floor of four bytes (`MinimumProductionChecksumWidth`): a width of one, two, or three is rejected, because below four bytes the per-decode masquerade bound becomes material at realistic difference sizes. **Breaking** for a caller constructing a width-one-through-three contract through the public constructor: move to a width of four or more (eight for a difference past the tens of thousands). The per-decode union-bound documentation on the decoder and on `ReconciliationContract.ChecksumWidth` now states that scaling explicitly and points at the decoder's new count and bound members.

- Verisync's tagging and pooled-memory primitives now come from `Lumoin.Base` rather than `Lumoin.Verisync.Core`. `VerisyncTags` and `TaggedMemory` build on and expose `Lumoin.Base.Tag`, the typed `Create<T>`/`With<T>`/`Get<T>` API with content-based, order-independent equality, in place of the former bespoke `Tag` record. The reconciliation cell buffer, item arena, dotted projection, encoder, decoder, and anti-entropy session now require an injected `MemoryPool<byte>`: the pool parameter is non-null, and the former pool-less convenience constructors and private heap-backed fallback owner are gone. Wire formats and reconciliation behavior are unchanged. Requires `Lumoin.Base` 0.0.4.

- Deserialization fails closed under one type across encodings: every `DeserializeMessageDelegate` the JSON and CBOR codecs build now throws a single new `MessageDeserializationException` (in `Lumoin.Verisync.Core`), with the encoding-native cause (a `JsonException`, a `CborContentException`, or a wrapped argument exception) preserved as its inner exception, so a channel consumer catches one type whether the wire is JSON or CBOR. Serializers are unchanged and still throw their native exceptions. This supersedes the surfaced exception type in the `JsonException` entries below; those now describe the inner cause a handler can still read off `InnerException`.

- The reconciliation decoder now peels in near-linear time. The previous decode re-scanned the whole decoded set on every absorbed symbol and re-scanned all cells to a fixpoint on every absorb, quadratic in the difference size, so throughput collapsed as differences grew. It now applies already-decoded items to incoming cells through an incremental cursor heap and finds newly pure cells through a work-list seeded by each modified cell. The decoded set, the completion point, the soundness rules, and the wire format are unchanged. Measured: a 16,384-item difference that took about 54 s now reconciles in about 0.2 s, and per-symbol throughput rises with the difference instead of falling.

- The encoder and decoder store coded symbols in a single flat, contiguous `ReconciliationCellBuffer` rather than parallel lists of per-cell arrays, so the XOR fold walks contiguous memory and the per-cell allocation is gone; the two duplicated content-keyed-bytes helpers are unified into one `ContentAddress`. `ReconciliationSymbol` gains an additive `ReadOnlySpan<byte>` constructor so a symbol can be snapshotted from a cell in a single copy. Behavior, wire format, and the pinned vectors are unchanged; the gain is locality and modest decode throughput.

- The encoder and decoder reuse their walk cursors instead of reallocating one per fold. The cursors that carry pending item contributions through the produce and peel priority queues were reference-type records cloned on every re-enqueue; they are now mutable structs stored inline in the queue and advanced in place, and the encoder cursor carries its checksum as a value re-materialized at fold time rather than a copied array. Behavior is byte-identical, and steady-state per-session allocation is down about 47% (about 287 KB to about 151 KB) with the live heap still flat.

- The reconciliation encoder, decoder, and `AntiEntropySession` are now `IDisposable` and rent their coded-cell backing from a caller-supplied `MemoryPool<byte>`, so the kernel's largest scoped buffer is accountable through the pool's rental metrics rather than a naked array. The constructors thread the required pool and a capacity hint; the session pre-sizes from its projected snapshot, disposes the encoder and decoder it owns, but never the injected pool. Pooled segments are cleared on rent, preserving the all-zero-fresh-cell contract the fold relies on, and the paired rent is exception-safe so a failed second rent returns the first rather than leaking it. A `ReconciliationSymbol` stays an owned-copy value: the documented exception for bytes that escape the kernel to callers and the wire.

### Removed

- The bespoke `Lumoin.Verisync.Core.Tag` type, superseded by `Lumoin.Base.Tag`. **Breaking** for any consumer that referenced `Lumoin.Verisync.Core.Tag` directly: the type moves to the `Lumoin.Base` namespace and drops the `(Type, object)` tuple factories, the `Data` property, and the `Type` indexer in favor of the typed `Create<T>`/`With<T>`/`Get<T>`/`TryGet<T>`/`Contains<T>` API. The internal heap-backed reconciliation fallback owner (`ReconciliationHeapMemoryOwner`) is removed in the same move.

- The pool-less and nullable-pool constructors on `ReconciliationCellBuffer`, `ReconciliationItemArena`, `DottedReconciliationProjection`, `ReconciliationEncoder`, `ReconciliationDecoder`, and `AntiEntropySession`. **Breaking**: these types now take a required, non-null `MemoryPool<byte>`, so a caller that previously omitted the pool (or passed `null`) must now name one explicitly, for example `BaseMemoryPool.Shared`.

### Fixed

- A remove-aware anti-entropy session can no longer poison its causal context on an interrupted exchange. The drain path used to fold the peer's context whenever the host wound the session down with `Complete()` and no apply had folded, but the fold could cover the dots of entries never transferred, and the next session would then classify those entries observed-and-removed: a permanent, cluster-wide false drop of live data. The drain now folds nothing on either role; a remove-aware initiator additionally defers its local drops while its fetch is outstanding, and a drop dispatched on a running initiator fails closed as an order violation. A wind-down before the exchange finishes is now observable as the new terminal `AntiEntropySessionState.Interrupted`, previously indistinguishable from `Completed`, and an interrupted session has folded no peer context at all. `Interrupted` and `IsConverged` are complementary: `IsConverged` stays false on any interrupted wind-down and true only through the reconciliation path. As a deliberate consequence, the responder's context advances only through applies during the exchange; the completion frame (see Added) converges both contexts in one session.
- The initiator's deferred local drops now apply after the fetch answer's elements, not before. Every applier folds the full peer context, and only the elements apply carries the entries that context covers, so the old order left a one-await window (the elements hook throwing, or the process dying between the two applies) where the folded context durably covered entries that never applied, and the next session would classify them observed-and-removed: the same permanent false drop the deferral itself exists to prevent, one handler step later. With the elements first, the fold always rides the hook call that carries the entries it covers, and a fault before the drops apply merely re-classifies them in the next session.
- A remove-aware responder now fails closed when the peer never ships its causal context: a done signal without a prior context faults the run, instead of the responder silently classifying later applies against the empty clock and completing as if the remove-aware exchange had happened. `SubmitAsync` and `TriggerBatchAsync` now document the `ChannelClosedException` their returned tasks fault with after `Complete()`.
- `RaftRunner` no longer strands proposals when its loop ends. A non-cancellation hook failure (the fail-closed path for a broken transport, durable store, or state machine) used to complete nothing: every queued, in-flight, and later-issued `ProposeAsync` task hung forever, and cancellation orphaned the proposal being dispatched between its dequeue and its result. On every early exit the runner now completes the work channel first and then cancels (on cancellation) or faults (on any other failure, with an `InvalidOperationException` carrying the loop failure as its inner exception) every pending and in-flight proposal, so a later `ProposeAsync`, `SubmitAsync`, or trigger fails fast with the now-documented `ChannelClosedException`. The `ProposeAsync` contract also states plainly that a faulted or cancelled proposal may already be appended and persisted, and may later commit, so a host that retries must tolerate or deduplicate a possible duplicate command.
- `RaftRunner.RunAsync` given a null `send` delegate now fails closed instead of hanging pre-enqueued proposals. The argument validation threw before the loop started and left the work channel writer open, so a proposal already queued on a runner that would never run hung forever; the null-send path now completes the writer and faults every enqueued proposal, with the validation failure as the inner exception, exactly as an early loop exit does.
- A `RaftRunner` proposal cancelled when the runner token ends the loop now carries that token as its cancellation cause. The abandonment used a token-less `TrySetCanceled`, so the resulting `TaskCanceledException` lost the attribution; it now passes the runner's cancellation token.
- `RaftRunner` no longer misreads a hook's own cancellation as a clean stop. The loop's `catch (OperationCanceledException)` is narrowed with `when (cancellationToken.IsCancellationRequested)`, so an `OperationCanceledException` a persist, send, or apply hook throws for its own reasons, while the runner token is not signalled, flows to the fault path and faults the pending proposals rather than cancelling them as if the runner had stopped cleanly.
- An add-only `AntiEntropySession` handed local drops by its difference resolver now fails closed with `InvalidOperationException` instead of dereferencing a missing drop applier. An add-only session wires no drop path, so a resolution carrying local drops used to `NullReferenceException` on the null-forgiving apply; it is now rejected at decode completion, mirroring the add-only rejection of the remove-aware context and drop frames.

### Security

- The reconciliation envelope JSON deserializer now fails closed as `JsonException` on every present-but-malformed field: a JSON-null hex field (which had leaked `ArgumentNullException`), a wrong-kind value where a string or hex field was expected (`InvalidOperationException`), a fractional or `Int32`-overflowing integer (`FormatException`), and a non-object or non-array carrier where the shape required one. Every `JsonElement` accessor is now kind-guarded before use, so a hostile or buggy peer can no longer drive the verifying deserializer to throw an exception type the contract promises it never will. A structurally missing required property fails closed as well: every per-field read across the JSON codecs (reconciliation, CRDT state, Raft, log commitment, consensus) now goes through a `RequireProperty` guard that throws rather than leaking the framework's `KeyNotFoundException`.

### Added

- The anti-entropy session closes its one-directional context gap with a wire-level completion frame. A remove-aware initiator whose exchange work completed sends `ReconciliationCompletion` as its final frame, carrying the count of transfer envelopes it sent; the responder, protected by add-only, role, and phase guards and a count check that fails closed on any transfer-count drift (a lost, truncated, or duplicated transfer envelope), runs its one terminal fold of the initiator's exchanged context and reaches `Completed`. One session now converges both members' contexts, where convergence previously required a second, reverse-direction session. An interrupted exchange emits no frame and keeps the landed semantics verbatim (`Interrupted`, zero folds, not converged). Remove-aware mode only; a 0.0.5 peer receiving the unknown frame tears down loudly through the uniform fail-closed deserialization contract.

- Sealing is group-quiescent-aware and its diagnostics are actionable. The offset strategy exposes an insert-quiescence probe: `OffsetAnchoredSequence.UnstableInserts(frontier)` returns the vertex insert-dots the frontier does not cover, in deterministic `(Replica, Counter)` order, surfaced through a new optional `SequenceUnstableInsertsDelegate` slot on the strategy context and as `CheckpointedSequence.UnstableInserts` (null for strategies whose compaction imposes no quiescence precondition). The container now refuses with two distinct diagnostics: `Seal` fails closed before any consensus round when the frontier leaves inserts uncovered, and `ApplyCommittedSeal`, after the digest check, tells a straggling writer the recovery: the committed frontier can never cover its in-flight inserts, so it adopts a healthy member's state, inherits the checkpoint and commitment by merging containers, and re-applies its edits as fresh inserts. Because a base-changing compaction re-identifies converted elements, offset appliers apply each base-changing committed seal exactly once (re-application fails closed; a drop-only seal re-applies idempotently exactly as RGA), and an offset member re-sealing after its own base-changing compaction is refused harmlessly at the equal-frontier arm. `OffsetAnchoredSequence.Merge` and `Rga.Merge` now fail closed when the operands carry conflicting vertices under one insert identity, since a dot mints exactly one immutable vertex; the check compares element values, so `TValue` must carry value equality under `EqualityComparer<T>.Default` (now a documented contract on both strategies). A drop-only offset compaction now keeps the prior base-offset translation map instead of replacing it, `TranslateAnchor` gains a matching identity fallback for unmapped in-range base offsets, and `FromState` rejects base-offset translations that target live anchors. The container docs state plainly that a member without fresh group-wide digests must never seal, and that the group's recovery from an island is wholesale adoption from it.

- The offset-anchored sequence strategy certifies removals on both of its axes, closing the same causal-invisibility defect the RGA strategies closed: `offset.v2` removes are dotted events that tick the context (live-tombstone removals as in the RGA mechanism, and base-offset removals through a per-offset remove-dot map), so gossip digests see remove divergence, the stability frontier certifies removals group-wide, and the strategy exposes the causal context and certified projection that make an offset container sealable. Compaction follows a four-way taxonomy: a stable tombstoned element whose remove is not yet certified converts into the new base as a pending-removed entry, never ghost-retained, carrying its remove-dots onto its new offset. The base carries a generation identity (the frontier it was materialized at, stamped only by base-changing compactions) and merges are fenced on it, because certified reclamation would let base value arrays repeat across genuinely different generations. Removed base entries are certified but not yet reclaimed: they stay as hidden ordering placeholders, and reclamation awaits a follow-on that carries a consensus-agreed reclamation set through the seal. Compaction, and therefore sealing an offset container, requires an insert-quiescent frontier that covers every vertex's insert-dot; an unstable vertex fails closed. Deserialization gains full parity validation, including one cross-axis dot pool, so a forged dot aliased between the live and base axes cannot turn an honest live certification into the reclamation of an unremoved base slot.

- The offset strategy's base addressing is generation-exact. `OffsetAddress`, the structural `OffsetAnchor` paired with the base generation it was read at, is the public addressing type on both sides of the surface: `InsertAfter` and `InsertAtHead` return it, and `Remove`, `TranslateAnchor`, and `VisibleElements` carry it, while `OffsetAnchor` stays internal to the strategy and its state model. The state gains a `baseGeneration` ordinal beside `baseFrontier`, stamped only by base-changing compactions and required fail-closed in the JSON. `TranslateAnchor` serves a base address by its generation: identity at the current generation, the base-offset map at exactly the one preceding generation the map serves, and `null` fail-closed for anything older or newer, so a stale or forged generation is refused rather than silently mis-served. Edits at a stale-generation base address fail closed with `ArgumentException`, the generation checked before the range; merges fence on the ordinal alongside the frontier. Head and live-dot addresses stay exact across generations and carry the canonical generation zero. The strategy identifier does not change.

- Sequence removes are dotted events the group can certify. `Rga<TValue>.Remove` now takes the removing `ReplicaId`, ticks the causal context, and records the minted remove-dot in a tombstone map (`target -> remove-dots`), so a remove enters the gossip digest and the stability frontier like any other event. Waterline compaction's drop gate additionally requires at least one remove-dot at or below the frontier, so a tombstone can no longer be dropped, and later resurrected by a laggard's merge, before every member has seen the removal. The compaction checkpoint is now the certified projection (the visible order filtered to stable insert-dots, excluding only elements whose remove is certified at the frontier), which makes it a pure function of the frontier and identical on every honest member. `Merge` fails closed, in both argument orders, when an operand presents an element live that the other operand's lineage compacted after a universally observed remove: such a state is a stale pre-remove replay and must adopt a current state wholesale. A new `CausalContext` accessor on `Rga<TValue>` (with a `SequenceCausalContextDelegate` seam on the strategy context and `CheckpointedSequence`) serves the digest path, which `ToState` could not on a compacted array. Tombstones loaded from pre-dotted state carry no remove-dot and are retained forever.

- Waterline compaction is driven by a consensus seal. `CheckpointedSequence.Seal` computes the certified projection at a caller-supplied stability frontier through a new strategy seam (`CertifySequenceProjectionDelegate`), proposes a frontier-carrying commitment through the CASPaxos register with a monotone change function (the proposal replaces the recovered commitment only when it is the first seal, strictly dominates the recovered frontier, or repeats it byte-identically; anything behind, concurrent, or equal-with-a-divergent-digest re-proposes the recovered value unchanged), and compacts only when its own proposal was chosen (`Sealed`), returning the container unchanged otherwise so a losing sealer retries above the winner. Committed frontiers therefore form a strictly ascending chain. Non-sealers converge through `ApplyCommittedSeal`, which verifies the member's own certified projection against the committed digest (a distinct diagnostic tells a lagging member to catch up first) and compacts on match. Checkpoints are dotted (`SequenceCheckpointEntry` pairs each value with its element identity), so a commitment is unambiguous under repeated values.

- `AntiEntropySession<TElement>.IsConverged`: a convergence attestation distinct from the terminal state alone. A wind-down before the exchange finishes now lands `AntiEntropySessionState.Interrupted` rather than `Completed`, and `IsConverged` agrees with that terminal split while additionally reading through pre-terminally for a responder still resolving fetches. It is set only on the reconciliation path (for an initiator when the decoder recovered the whole symmetric difference and the resolution finished, for a responder when the peer's done signal attested a complete decode against this session's snapshot) and stays false for a session merely terminated. It is written only by the consumer loop and read with a volatile-safe read, so a host may poll it from another thread alongside `State` to assert the sets actually converged.

- The reconciliation decoder's per-decode false-peel bound is now observable. `ReconciliationDecoder.PurityCheckCount` counts every non-neutral purity evaluation a decode runs (the masquerade opportunities, each accepting a mixed cell as pure with probability `2^(-8 * ChecksumWidth)`), and `FalseDecodeProbabilityBound` is that count times the per-cell bound, clamped to one: the operative union-bound figure over the whole decode, distinct from the per-cell probability. A consumer acting on a decode, such as a repair path, should require the bound far below one before trusting the recovered difference; the bound is against random corruption, an adversary holding the checksum key being covered by the contract's key discipline.

- Pool-aware and item-stream wire deserialization, the read-side companions to the message channel. `OwnedMessageChannelReader<TMessage>` deserializes each framed message into an owned, disposable value whose bytes are rented from a required, injected `MemoryPool<byte>` through the new `DeserializeOwnedMessageDelegate<TMessage>` rather than copied to the GC heap; ownership of each yielded value transfers to the consumer, which disposes it. `ItemStreamChannelReader<TItem>` reads a length-prefixed flow of one structured type and drives a per-item handler (`DecodeItemDelegate<TItem>` plus the synchronous `ItemHandlerDelegate<TItem>`) materialising no collection: each item is borrowed for exactly one handler call and the pooled backing the decoder rented for it is released the moment the call returns. Both readers reuse the existing channel's framing, padding, and hostile-frame bounds, now centralised in one internal `FrameReader` (the outer-length cap, the padded inner real-length check, the up-front item-count bound, and the no-trailing-bytes rule). The plain `MessageChannelReader<TMessage>`, refactored onto the shared framing, and the entire write side are unchanged.

- Remove-aware (dot-cloud) reconciliation atop the anti-entropy session: an `OrSet` or `DottedVersionVectorSet` reconciles its observed removes as well as its adds, with reconcile-then-apply equal to `DottedVersionVectorSet.Merge` while bytes on the wire stay proportional to the divergence. The kernel, encoder, decoder, and symbol stay an unchanged generic digest-set engine; remove-awareness is host-side. The session takes an optional pinned local `VectorClockState`; the initiator projects present `(dot, value)` entries to digests through a new `DottedReconciliationProjection`, decodes the symmetric difference, and classifies each decoded dot against the peer's exchanged causal context by the merge rule (a held dot the peer's context covers is a local drop; an absent dot the initiator's own pre-session context covers is pushed as a drop rather than re-added, the resurrection guard). The genuinely new wire surface is a whole-context exchange (`ReconciliationContext`, shipped whole and never reconciled) and a remove push (`ReconciliationDrop`, dots only); a `null` local context keeps the add-only path byte-identical.

- SIMD acceleration for the reconciliation kernel's hot loops: the byte-wise XOR folds and neutrality scans behind encoding, peeling, and symbol combination now route through `ReconciliationXor`, a facade over per-width vector backends (`Vector128`, `Vector256`, `Vector512`, with a scalar reference) selected by a dispatch that the JIT folds to a direct call. The width tiers are cross-platform, lowering to SSE/AVX2/AVX-512, NEON, and WASM SIMD alike, and the wire contract is unchanged: every backend is pinned byte-identical to the scalar reference across edge lengths. The benchmarks project gains XOR and encoder throughput benchmarks plus `--reconciliation-overhead`, a seed-pinned measurement of bytes-on-wire against the information-theoretic floor and full-state and hash-list anchor rows (symbols per difference converge to about 1.37x at a thousand-item divergence, three orders of magnitude under either anchor at small differences).

- `AntiEntropySession`: the host-side runner for one point-to-point reconciliation session, in the `RaftRunner` production shape, where all inbound work flows through a single-consumer queue, so every state change and every outbound send happens on one loop and transport writes are serialized by construction. A session pins one set version (the item snapshot is copied and encoded at construction); the initiator decodes against its own lockstep encoder, signals done, and classifies the difference through `ResolveReconciliationDifferenceDelegate` into fetches and pushes; the responder streams batches only on host `TriggerBatchAsync` calls (liveness stays external, no timers, no entropy), serves fetches through `ServeReconciliationFetchDelegate` with exact-coverage verification, and applies elements through `ApplyReconciliationElementsDelegate`. Every protocol violation (mismatched offer, out-of-role frame, stream gap, partial fetch answer) fails the session closed. `ReconciliationEnvelope` gains the same exactly-one-payload dispatch guard as the Raft envelope. Element-level reconciliation is add-only at this base tier; remove-aware reconciliation over dot-cloud causal contexts is added separately in this release.

- The reconciliation wire layer: a `RaftEnvelope`-style one-of-five message family (`ReconciliationEnvelope` carrying offer, symbol batch, done, fetch, or elements) and `ReconciliationJson` codecs following the established fail-closed conventions. The deserializer is verifying: it pins the local `ReconciliationContract` and rejects an offer that does not match it, so a contract mismatch throws before any symbol is absorbed, and validates every hex field's width against that contract. The offer never carries key bytes; it carries a key check (a PRF tag over a fixed public input) so peers with differing checksum keys abort up front instead of failing to peel.

- The rateless set-reconciliation kernel: a replica encodes a set of fixed-width items into an unbounded coded-symbol stream (`ReconciliationEncoder`) whose symbol-wise XOR with a peer's stream is the stream of their symmetric difference, recovered by a peeling decoder (`ReconciliationDecoder`) from a prefix proportional to the difference size. Neither side ever sizes the divergence, and an equal-set reconciliation completes on the first symbol. The encoding is a group homomorphism from (sets, symmetric difference) to (streams, XOR). `ReconciliationContract` pins what peers must agree on before subtraction is meaningful (item domain, item width, checksum width and key; checksum width bounds the masquerade probability, and a secret key turns a poisoned stream into detected-and-aborted); injectivity enforcement is local-only (`ReconciliationInjectivityEnforcement`), and `ProjectReconciliationItemsDelegate` is the seam that projects a pinned state snapshot to reconcilable items. Wire codecs, the session runner, and SIMD XOR backends are later phases.

  are accepted only while they equal the acceptor's promise and prepares are classic-only, so only the
  pre-promised initial fast round is blind-writable. Retry a contended fast write with the same fast

- `TaggedMemory` equality members fail closed after dispose; the memory pool validates slab sizing
  before allocation (no more wrapped products), disposes its rental activity on a throwing rent,
  makes owner double-dispose idempotent, and keeps the active-rentals gauge paired across pool
  shutdown races.

  (16 MiB default) instead of buffering toward a hostile four-byte length prefix;
  `MessageChannelWriter` fails fast on oversized payloads. The reader completes its pipe even when
  deserialization throws.
- The JSON CRDT-state deserializers validate untrusted input: replica ids must decode to exactly

  `RaftNodeState` (the Figure 2 durable triple, validated fail-closed on restore; a restored node is
  a follower at commit zero rediscovering the volatile commit index), `RaftRunner` — a
  single-consumer message-driven loop preserving the node's single-threaded contract with the
  universal handle → persist → apply → send sequencing, named seams
  (`SendRaftEnvelopeDelegate`, `PersistRaftStateDelegate`, `ApplyCommittedDelegate`), host-triggered
  elections and heartbeats (still no timers or entropy in the library), propose with a faulting task
  on non-leaders, and self-quiescing follower catch-up off append replies — plus `RaftJson` wire
  codecs for the envelope and the durable state, mirroring the consensus message codecs' strictness.
  Apply is exactly-once per process lifetime and at-least-once across restarts, documented on the
  seam. Proven over real localhost sockets: election, replication, identical applied sequences on
  every node, and a follower restarted from its persisted state converging after reconnect.
- A naive, safety-correct, in-memory Raft node (`RaftNode<TCommand>` and its message records): the
  complete Figure 2 safety core — election restriction, log matching with conflict truncation and
  idempotent re-delivery, and the current-term commit rule — with liveness explicitly external
  (host-triggered elections, no timers). The within-trust-domain log-replication primitive
  complementing the register (metadata-grade anchors) and the CRDT plane (no ordering). The test
  suite was authored blind against the same specification and includes the Figure 8 scenario.
- `StabilityFrontier`, `CompactSequenceDelegate`, `TranslateAnchorDelegate`, and a `Compact` step on
  `CheckpointedSequence`: the waterline seams — the frontier (element-wise group minimum, in-library
  because a silent member must pin the floor) is the WHEN of compaction, the agreed checkpoint the
  WHAT; the first two compaction laws wait in the shared harness for the first compacting strategy.
- A shared sequence-strategy law harness in the test suite: every registered strategy inherits the
  join-semilattice laws, merge-order convergence, and local intention preservation as property tests
  exercised through the strategy context — the contract future strategies (including the compaction
  strategies) are built against. The RGA strategy is the first registration.
- `PersistAcceptorDelegate<TValue>` and an optional durability hook on `ConsensusNode.RunAsync`:
  when supplied, the acceptor state is made durable after every state-changing request and before the
  matching reply is sent, so an unpersisted promise is never observable — the documented crash-safety
  contract is now a seam instead of a caller obligation. A throwing hook fail-closes the reply.
- `SequenceCrdtContext<TSequence, TValue, TAnchor>` with named operation delegates and
  `WellKnownSequenceStrategies`: the sequence CRDT behind `CheckpointedSequence` is now pluggable.
  A strategy (addressing model, merge, ordering) is injected as a delegate bundle and named by a
  stable identifier that is part of the document's replication contract — merging containers carrying
  different strategy identifiers fails closed, since mismatched strategies silently diverge.
  `CheckpointedSequence<TValue>` became `CheckpointedSequence<TSequence, TValue, TAnchor>` created
  via `Create(context)`; the RGA-backed strategy (`verisync.sequence.rga.v1`) is the first
  registration and preserves the previous behavior exactly.
- `FramePadding`: opt-in padded framing for the message channel. Frames are padded up to size
  buckets (powers-of-two or fixed ladders) with an inner real-length prefix, so a network observer
  cannot distinguish message types or content sizes within a bucket — a metadata-privacy measure
  from the sealed-segments design. The unpadded wire format is unchanged byte for byte; both
  endpoints must share the policy, and the attacker-influenced inner length is never trusted past
  the frame bounds.
- `LogCommitmentJson`: hand-written AOT-safe JSON codecs for `LogHead`, `MerkleInclusionProof`,
  `MerkleConsistencyProof`, and `SegmentSeal<TProof>`, so heads, proofs, and seals can cross a
  message channel. The seal deserializer re-derives the canonical bytes and digest through
  `SegmentSeal.Create` and fails closed on a digest mismatch, so a tampered seal is rejected at the
  codec.
- `MerkleFrontier`: the bounded-state companion to `MerkleLogTree` — an append-only root tracker
  holding only the O(log n) frontier peaks, producing roots byte-identical to the full tree (verified
  by an exhaustive size sweep against it). Proofs still come from `MerkleLogTree` over archived
  leaves; the frontier is how a live replica tracks its root without holding history.
- `LogHead` and `LogHeadConsistency`: the log-plane anti-equivocation exchange. A head claims a tree
  size and root; `LogHeadConsistency.Verify` binds a `MerkleConsistencyProof` to exactly the two
  heads before trusting it and reduces a failure between authentic heads to portable fork evidence.
  In the single-tree composition a `SegmentSeal` whose commitment is the tree root at its boundary is
  an attested head, so consecutive seals prove their own consistency.
- `SegmentSeal<TProof>` with `AttestSealDelegate` and `VerifySealAttestationDelegate`: sealed log
  segments — a versioned, byte-for-byte pinned commitment to a contiguous entry range, chained by seal
  digest with in-library link verification (digest linkage, index continuity, genesis rules).
  Attestation evidence covers the seal digest and rides outside the digested bytes. Combined with an
  `AuthenticatedRegister` whose accumulator is a `MerkleLogTree` folding entry digests, sealing a
  segment is reading the accumulator root, and per-entry membership in a sealed segment is provable
  with a `MerkleInclusionProof` alone.
- `MerkleLogTree`, `MerkleInclusionProof`, and `MerkleConsistencyProof`: an RFC 9162 (Certificate
  Transparency v2) append-only Merkle log tree with inclusion and consistency proofs, hash-agnostic
  through `ComputeDigestDelegate` with structural `0x00`/`0x01` domain separation. The documented
  byte layout is a cross-stack contract; the test suite pins the canonical RFC 6962 SHA-256 vectors.
  This is the foundation for sealed log segments and bounded logs.
- `VectorClock.IncrementPastAll`: a Lamport-style advance whose new counter exceeds every observed
  counter.
- Documented contracts: `ConsensusNode` durability obligations across restarts, the CASPaxos
  "a failed change may still be chosen" retry caveat on `ChangeOutcome`, the full verification
  obligations of `VerifyChainIntegrityDelegate`, and the non-associativity of the
  `DottedVersionVector` dot component.
